# Research Philosophy of the SUDAT Programme

## Core Principles

The Scalar Unified Dark Adaptive Theory (SUDAT) Research Programme is built upon a set of foundational principles that guide every aspect of this project, from theoretical development to numerical implementation and publication. These principles are not mere aspirations; they are the operational standards we apply to ensure that our work contributes meaningfully to the scientific discourse.

### 1. Scientific Honesty

We commit to representing our results accurately and without embellishment. This repository is a documentation of where our current understanding stands—not a marketing exercise. We explicitly state what the theory can and cannot do, and we do not claim empirical successes that have not been rigorously demonstrated. Negative results are recorded and shared with the same care as positive ones.

### 2. Preserve Validated Physics

Any modification to the known laws of physics must respect the robust experimental and observational constraints already established. Our effective theory is constructed to smoothly recover the standard behaviour in the appropriate limits (e.g., low densities, early times). We do not discard well‑tested physics; we explore extensions that respect it.

### 3. Physics Before Fitting

Parameter tuning to achieve a good fit to data is a legitimate part of model building, but it is not the primary driver of our research. The core focus is on understanding the underlying physical mechanisms—the dynamics of the vacuum, the nature of symmetry breaking, and the emergence of cosmological phases. We prioritise physical insight over numerical optimisation, and we refrain from adding degrees of freedom solely to improve the fit.

### 4. Derivation Before Parameter Tuning

Effective theories are valuable, but they are not the final word. Our goal is to derive the scalar potential and its environmental coupling from a more fundamental theoretical framework. The parameters in the current effective model are placeholders for deeper physics. We do not treat them as free parameters to be arbitrarily adjusted; instead, we explore the qualitative features they produce and use those insights to guide the search for a more fundamental formulation.

### 5. Negative Results Are Scientific Results

A model that does not reproduce observations is not a failure—it is a constraint. The fact that the current SUDAT effective theory does not match the ΛCDM expansion history is a significant piece of information. It tells us that either the underlying assumptions are incomplete, the effective parametrisation is insufficient, or the physics must be modified. We publish and share these negative results openly because they are essential for the scientific community to avoid dead ends and build upon robust foundations.

### 6. Frozen Benchmark Philosophy

Version 1.0.0 of this repository is frozen. It represents the exact state of the effective theory as described in the accompanying manuscript. By freezing this version, we provide a stable reference point against which all future developments—whether they are theoretical refinements, numerical improvements, or alternative implementations—can be compared. This ensures that progress is measurable and that the community can trust the reproducibility of our work.

### 7. Reproducibility

Every result presented in this repository is fully reproducible. We provide the complete source code, the exact parameter sets, and the necessary environment specifications. The `results/` directory contains the raw outputs and figures generated for the release. Researchers can run the same scripts and obtain identical results, facilitating verification and independent analysis.

### 8. Open Science

All materials—code, data, documentation, and manuscripts—are made freely available under open licenses. We believe that science advances most rapidly when knowledge is shared without barriers. We encourage peer review, collaboration, and the use of our work in other studies, under the terms of the Apache License 2.0.

## Why Version 1.0.0 Is Frozen

The decision to freeze this release is deliberate. It serves multiple purposes:

- **Stability**: Researchers can rely on a fixed baseline for their own investigations without wondering if the underlying code has changed.
- **Accountability**: Any claims made in the manuscript can be traced back to a specific version of the code.
- **Progression**: Future versions (v2.0, v3.0, etc.) will be explicitly linked to theoretical advances, not to ad hoc modifications. This encourages disciplined, theory‑driven evolution.

## Future Generations Must Outperform Through Stronger Theory, Not Arbitrary Tuning

The SUDAT programme does not aim to win a curve‑fitting competition. Our objective is to develop a physically motivated model that naturally explains the dark sector. If future iterations of the theory are to improve upon the expansion history, they must do so because the underlying physics is richer and more accurate—not because we have introduced extra free parameters or adjusted existing ones to match data.

We therefore hold that:

- The current effective theory is a necessary first step.
- Its limitations are opportunities to learn.
- Any future improvements must be justified by theoretical arguments and validated by independent consistency checks, not merely by a better χ² value.

This philosophy ensures that the SUDAT programme remains a serious scientific endeavour, grounded in the principles of theoretical physics and computational cosmology.

The purpose of Version 1 is not to provide the final form of SUDAT, but to establish a transparent, reproducible scientific baseline from which all future developments can be objectively evaluated.
